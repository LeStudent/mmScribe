mmScribe: Understanding Human Motion from Millimeter-Wave Point Cloud Sequences

Lin Wang3, Jiliang Wang1,†

1Tsinghua University    2Taiyuan University of Technology    3Yanshan University

Paper Code & Data
Abstract:   Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar is a promising modality for privacy-preserving and robust Human Activity Recognition (HAR). However, the potential of current mmWave systems is severely constrained by their rigid classification-based formulation. By reducing complex human behaviors to a finite set of predefined labels (e.g., sitting or walking), these methods fail to capture the intrinsic continuity, transitions, and contextual dependencies of real-world actions. We present mmScribe, the first end-to-real human motion understanding framework that transforms sparse mmWave point clouds into fine-grained natural language descriptions. To bridge the gap between RF signals and Large Language Models (LLMs), we introduce three innovations: (1) A spatio-temporal masked autoencoder that exploits motion continuity to extract robust dynamics from sparse radar signal; (2) A coarse-to-fine alignment strategy that decouples feature projection from semantic tuning to prevent catastrophic forgetting in LLMs; and (3) A physics-informed hybrid synthesis pipeline fusing ray-traced reflections with real-world clutter to enable robust sim-to-real transfer. We implement mmScribe on COTS mmWave radars and conduct extensive real-world experiments. The results demonstrate mmScribe outperformed the state-of-the-arts by 22.44% in SimCSE and 6.89% in Bertscore for real collected data, marking a paradigm shift from rigid activity recognition to open-vocabulary motion understanding.
